Food for Thought • FIBA World Cup Final

Gold Is Earned Twice.

France won the opening argument. Team USA won the adjustment. A 14-point hole became an 18-point championship victory because experience did not panic when pressure arrived.

UNITED STATESWORLD CHAMPIONS
97–79
FRANCEWORLD SILVER

The final was two different games.

For roughly a quarter and a half, France looked like the team that had solved the tournament. The spacing was clean, the threes were falling, and Gabby Williams powered a 21–2 burst that pushed the lead to 14. Then the United States stopped chasing the game and started controlling it.

What went right, and what went wrong?

USA: What went right

  • No panic. The veterans answered France's 14-point lead possession by possession instead of trying to erase it with one shot.
  • The halftime bridge. Clark's three at the horn cut the deficit to two and changed the emotional temperature of the game.
  • The starting group took command. Stewart, Young, Copper and Gray combined scoring with steady decision-making.
  • Rebounding traveled. A 36–27 edge gave USA second chances and helped prevent France from restarting its transition attack.
  • The defense adjusted. France scored only 12 in the third as USA disrupted the rhythm that fueled the early run.

USA: What went wrong

  • The opening defense was reactive. France found driving lanes, kick-outs and early threes before USA established its physicality.
  • Ball security wobbled. Clark finished with four turnovers, including early possessions where pressure held the ball in one place too long.
  • The rotation tightened sharply. The veteran core carried heavy minutes while several young players had only brief opportunities.
  • Free throws left points available. Reese's misses were part of an uneven night at the line that could matter in a closer rematch.

France: What went right

  • The game plan worked early. France spaced the floor, attacked decisively and landed the first true punch with a 21–2 run.
  • Williams bent the defense. Her 20 points and seven assists forced USA to account for both her scoring and playmaking.
  • Malonga kept growing into the moment. The 20-year-old center finished with a team-high 21 points and scored through the final stretch.
  • France proved it belongs. Its first World Cup final ended with the nation's best finish, not a failed tournament.

France: What went wrong

  • The offense became harder to reproduce. The early shotmaking did not survive USA's defensive adjustment.
  • The third quarter broke the structure. A 30–12 period erased the lead and forced France to play from urgency rather than flow.
  • The glass tilted the possession count. France lost the rebound battle by nine and could not consistently end USA possessions.
  • The supporting scoring thinned. Williams and Malonga combined for 41, but the rest of France produced only 38.
Food for Thought

France showed that USA can be moved. USA showed that moving a champion and finishing one are still two different assignments.

The 97–79 score looks comfortable. The path was not. That is what made this final useful: USA's crown is intact, but the world is no longer waiting politely outside the throne room.
