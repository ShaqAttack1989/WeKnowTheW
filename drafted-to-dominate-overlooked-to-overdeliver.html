Every April, families celebrate and franchises introduce tomorrow's cornerstones. The WNBA Draft is a prediction, though, not a promise. A No. 1 pick can be gone in two seasons. An undrafted guard can rewrite the rookie record book.

Who gets labeled a bust? The player whose college dominance never translated? The front office that picked her? Or a development system with too few roster spots and too little patience? The global game provides an essential second opinion.

How we're judging: Draft disappointment measures WNBA return against selection cost, not whether a player has talent, value as a person, or a successful career overseas. Injuries, player development, championships and national-team accomplishments all matter.

PART ONE · THE HIGH PICKS

Big Draft Capital. Small WNBA Returns.

Not all seven situations are alike. Some players were given little time; others suffered serious injuries. Several turned their careers around abroad. The draft positions, however, still invite scrutiny.

DRAFT DISAPPOINTMENT

Charli Collier

2021 · No. 1 · Dallas Wings

45 games · 2.9 PPG

Dallas drafted the Texas standout to anchor its frontcourt. Her All-Rookie selection offered an encouraging start, but she averaged only 2.9 points and 2.5 rebounds across two WNBA seasons. A franchise-defining pick never became a franchise-defining player.

Where are they now? Her basketball story continued overseas. After professional stops in Italy, Israel, Poland, China and Australia, she joined Melikgazi Kayseri in Turkey for 2025–26 and İstanbul Gençlik Spor for 2026–27.

A clear No. 1-pick disappointment, not the end of a pro career.

DRAFT DISAPPOINTMENT

Evelyn Akhator

2017 · No. 3 · Dallas Wings

15 games · 0.9 PPG

Akhator barely got started in Dallas: 15 appearances and only 61 WNBA minutes. No. 3 overall was an enormous investment for that return. Yet judging this Nigerian international by her WNBA box score alone would miss most of her résumé.

Where are they now? She joined BOTAŞ in Turkey for 2026–27 after competing in Spain and France. Akhator averaged 12.6 points and 9.0 rebounds at the 2018 FIBA Women's World Cup for Nigeria and has been an important AfroBasket contributor.

The draft pick failed. The international player did not.

DRAFT DISAPPOINTMENT

Lauren Cox

2020 · No. 3 · Indiana Fever

41 games · 2.9 PPG

The Baylor champion was expected to provide size, defense and passing. Instead, injuries and limited opportunities kept Cox from securing a sustained WNBA role in Indiana or Los Angeles.

Where are they now? She later played productively with the Townsville Fire in Australia's WNBL, including double-doubles in 2024–25. A knee injury interrupted 2025–26; a new 2026–27 team has not been verified.

An injury-affected lottery disappointment, not a fair case for blaming talent alone.

DRAFT DISAPPOINTMENT

Kysre Gondrezick

2021 · No. 4 · Indiana Fever

24 games · 1.7 PPG

Indiana's surprise top-four selection never secured a regular rotation role. Gondrezick made 19 appearances for the Fever and five more for Chicago in 2024, with little statistical return from a valuable lottery pick.

Where are they now? She has pursued fashion, entertainment and a basketball comeback. Athletes Unlimited announced her for 2026, but a serious Achilles injury affected her return. No current WNBA or overseas club contract has been verified.

An especially costly scouting gamble for Indiana.

DRAFT DISAPPOINTMENT

Chelsea Dungee

2021 · No. 5 · Dallas Wings

14 games · 0.6 PPG

Dungee scored 22.3 points per game as an Arkansas senior. Dallas selected her fifth, then played her for approximately 65 total WNBA minutes. Was that a failed evaluation, a failed developmental fit, or both?

Where are they now? After professional basketball in Turkey and Australia, Chelsea Dungee-Artberry moved into college coaching. For 2026–27 she is at Alabama in player development, recruiting operations and an assistant-coaching role.

A poor draft return, followed by a promising second act in coaching.

DRAFT DISAPPOINTMENT

Alaina Coates

2017 · No. 2 · Chicago Sky

95 games · 2.7 PPG

A foot and ankle injury disrupted the South Carolina star's WNBA launch. Coates never averaged the kind of production expected from the second overall pick, though she later earned a championship ring with Las Vegas in 2023.

Where are they now? She played in the 2026 Athletes Unlimited season. Overseas stops include Hungary, China, Turkey, Israel and Italy; at Nesibe Aydın in 2021–22 she averaged 17.9 points and 12.9 rebounds.

Underwhelming lottery return; meaningful global longevity.

DRAFT DISAPPOINTMENT

Kaleena Mosqueda-Lewis

2015 · No. 3 · Seattle Storm

157 games · 5.1 PPG

One of UConn's most accomplished shooters did not become the offensive centerpiece Seattle hoped for. Across her WNBA career, her three-point accuracy was 33.7%, well below the reputation she carried into the draft. She did, however, win the 2018 title with Seattle.

Where are they now? She continued her career in Europe and returned to Toulouse Métropole Basket in France in early 2025 after a reported retirement. A 2026–27 club has not been confirmed.

A below-expectations No. 3 pick, not a total career failure.

PART TWO · THE HIDDEN GEMS

Passed Over. Never Counted Out.

Some went undrafted. Others were selected late and had to wait years for opportunities. Every one of these careers shows why draft-night rankings aren't destiny.

OVERRATED THE DRAFT

Sydney Taylor

Undrafted · 2024 · Chicago Sky

2026: 504 points · 13.6 PPG

No draft-night phone call. Taylor instead played in Lithuania and Poland, developing an experienced scorer's game. She earned a 2026 Chicago training-camp invitation and became an AP All-Rookie selection.

Where are they now? She finished the 2026 season with Chicago as the highest-scoring undrafted rookie in WNBA history: 504 points in 37 games, with two game-winning go-ahead threes. She is a proven WNBA scorer entering her next career decision.

A historic undrafted rookie season. The headline of the other side of this debate.

OVERRATED THE DRAFT

Becky Hammon

Undrafted · 1999 · New York Liberty

6× All-Star · Hall of Fame

The league let the Colorado State star go unpicked. New York signed her, and she became a six-time WNBA All-Star, earning four All-WNBA honors across a 16-season career.

Where are they now? After starring internationally in Russia and winning an Olympic bronze for Russia in 2008, Hammon became Las Vegas Aces head coach. She led titles in 2022, 2023 and 2025 and entered the Basketball Hall of Fame in 2023.

From undrafted to Hall of Fame: the gold standard.

Erica Wheeler

Undrafted · 2013 · Rutgers

2019 All-Star Game MVP

Wheeler took the overseas route before breaking into the WNBA in 2015. She built herself into Indiana's starting point guard and became the first undrafted player to win WNBA All-Star Game MVP.

Where are they now? She played for the Los Angeles Sparks in 2026, averaging 9.0 points and 5.0 assists. Her time in overseas leagues was a bridge to the WNBA, not the consolation prize.

An All-Star whose story no draft board predicted.

OVERRATED THE DRAFT

Kayla Thornton

Undrafted · 2014 · UTEP

2024 champion · 2025 All-Star

Defense, rebounding, energy and adaptability are easy to underprice on draft night. Thornton turned those traits into a title with the New York Liberty in 2024 and became Golden State's first All-Star in 2025.

Where are they now? Thornton helped lead the Valkyries into the 2026 WNBA Finals. International work includes Australia, Israel, Italy, Puerto Rico, Russia, Turkey and South Korea, where she won league honors.

The kind of cornerstone that costs zero draft capital.

Alysha Clark

2010 · No. 17 · San Antonio

3× champion · Sixth Player of the Year

Being picked in the second round did not guarantee Clark a job. San Antonio waived her twice. Seattle eventually gave her a role, and she evolved into an elite defender and championship contributor in 2018, 2020 and 2023.

Where are they now? Clark played for Dallas in 2026, scoring 21 in its September 30 playoff win over Golden State. Her long international career has included Israel, Turkey, Poland and France.

Three rings and a Sixth Player award from a discarded second-rounder.

Pauline Astier

Undrafted · France · New York Liberty

2026: 9.3 PPG · 3.3 APG

Undrafted did not mean inexperienced. Astier came to New York in 2026 with years of high-level European club basketball behind her and a silver medal with France at the 2024 Olympics.

Where are they now? After contributing 10.2 points and 5.6 assists in EuroLeague for USK Praha in 2025–26, she became a useful Liberty guard during the 2026 WNBA season and playoff run.

An American draft miss, not an international scouting mystery.

Oct. 7 playoffs: 20 points

The Liberty chose Fauthoux late in the third round in 2021 and waited five years for her WNBA debut. She spent that time building a serious professional résumé in Europe and with the French national team.

Where are they now? Fauthoux joined New York in 2026. Her four threes and 20 points against Atlanta on October 7 illustrated why patience and a third-round investment can pay off, even if a definitive WNBA career verdict is premature.

Was the 2021 Draft the Ultimate Warning?

Dallas drafted Charli Collier first and Chelsea Dungee fifth; Indiana took Kysre Gondrezick fourth. Those three combined for just 83 WNBA games. That return on three top-five choices is difficult to defend. But it doesn't automatically invalidate the entire 2021 class.

The point is what teams do after the announcement. Was the skill evaluation wrong? Was the system a poor fit? Were there enough minutes to develop an answer? These are decisions a general manager must own.

The International Game Is Not a Footnote

Evelyn Akhator's Nigerian national-team work and Turkish career tell a dramatically different story than her 15 WNBA games. Charli Collier, Lauren Cox and Alaina Coates also found playing opportunities outside the United States. Overseas leagues give some players extended roles, a different pace and the chance to build confidence that an 11th or 12th WNBA roster spot rarely affords.

And that road also runs in reverse. Sydney Taylor developed in Lithuania and Poland before her historic 2026 Chicago season. Erica Wheeler built her base internationally. Pauline Astier arrived in New York as an experienced European professional, not an inexperienced prospect. Kayla Thornton turned years of global hoops into championship credentials.

So Who Really Missed?

Charli Collier has a strong case as the biggest modern No. 1 disappointment. Evelyn Akhator presents the most extreme gap between WNBA draft cost and minutes. The injury histories of Lauren Cox and Alaina Coates call for nuance.

On the other side, Becky Hammon remains the historical standard for an undrafted WNBA success, while Sydney Taylor delivers the newest example. Draft position tells us what a team believed in April. A career tells us what the player became.

WE KNOW THE W VERDICT: The scouting report isn't the final report. And a player's story does not end when a team stops calling.
