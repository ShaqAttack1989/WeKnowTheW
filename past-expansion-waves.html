WAVE ONE

Eight teams became sixteen in four seasons.

The WNBA launched in 1997 with eight charter teams. Then it expanded aggressively: Detroit and Washington in 1998, Minnesota and Orlando in 1999, and Indiana, Miami, Portland and Seattle in 2000.

WAVE TWO + THE LONG PAUSE

Growth slowed, but it did not stop.

2006

Chicago Sky

Chicago joined as a new expansion franchise and became one of the league's early independently operated teams.

2008

Atlanta Dream

Atlanta joined for the 2008 season. The league would not add another expansion franchise for 17 years.

2009–2024

Expansion pause

The map still changed through relocations and franchise transitions, but no brand-new expansion team entered after Atlanta until Golden State.

Important distinction: relocation is not the same as expansion. Detroit's old Shock franchise eventually became today's Dallas Wings; the 2029 Detroit team is a separate new expansion franchise.

THE MODERN BOOM

The drought ended — then the floodgates opened.

2025

Golden State Valkyries

The Valkyries became the first new WNBA expansion franchise since Atlanta in 2008.

2026

Toronto Tempo + Portland Fire

Toronto became the league's first team outside the United States, while Portland returned to the WNBA map with a revived Fire identity.

2028–2030

Cleveland → Detroit → Philadelphia

The league's next approved wave takes the W to 18 teams, one new franchise per season across 2028, 2029 and 2030.
