THE RATING

How the W Score works

The W Score is a We Know the W editorial rating, not an official league statistic. Peak matters, but it does not erase longevity. Rings matter, but context matters too. Defense is not a footnote, and neither is the ability to thrive beside other stars.

25Peak dominance
20Winning and playoff translation
20Two way impact
15Longevity
10Role portability
10League imprint

Why no perfect 100? The league is still writing its history. The top of the scale should leave room for the next chapter.

THE FIRST UNIT

Shak’s All Time Starting Five

Bird organizes. Taurasi bends the floor. Moore connects everything. Wilson attacks the present. Leslie protects the paint and represents the foundation.

97.8

Sue Bird

PG · Bridge Era · Seattle Storm

Every superteam needs someone who sees the next advantage before it appears. Bird owns the league career assists record, won four championships and could control pace without hijacking possessions.

99.2

Diana Taurasi

SG · Bridge to Modern · Phoenix Mercury

The all time scoring leader supplies range, audacity and late game nerve. This lineup does not need her to create every shot. That makes her even more dangerous.

98.1

Maya Moore

SF · Dynasty Era · Minnesota Lynx

Moore is the connector who can also become the best player on the floor. She scores from every level, defends across the perimeter and understands how to win inside a loaded lineup.

99.5

A’ja Wilson

PF · Modern Era · Las Vegas Aces

Wilson is the frontcourt answer to almost every question. She can erase mistakes at the rim, switch onto smaller players and carry a postseason offense. In 2025, she became the first four time MVP in league history.

98.4

Lisa Leslie

C · Foundation Era · Los Angeles Sparks

Leslie gives the group size, touch, rim protection and foundational greatness. She won three MVP awards and two championships, and delivered the first dunk in WNBA history.

FOOD FOR THOUGHT

Recency bias does not get to run the fast break, but nostalgia does not get an automatic starting spot either.

The hardest part is not naming greatness. It is deciding what kind of greatness a team needs. The W Score gives us a common language. The roster gives us an argument. Thirty seasons of WNBA history give us more than enough greatness for both.
