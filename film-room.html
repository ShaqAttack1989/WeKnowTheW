START WITH THE WHY

The shot is the ending. The story starts earlier.

01 · POSITIONS

Five starting spots. Far more than five jobs.

Traditional positions are a helpful starting point, but modern lineups are fluid. A forward may initiate the offense, a guard may screen, and a center may stretch the floor or make decisions from the elbow.

Positionless does not mean jobless.It means the lineup can assign ballhandling, screening, spacing, rebounding and defensive responsibilities by skill rather than height alone.

02 · OFFENSE

Create an advantage. Make the defense choose.

Good offense is not just making hard shots. It uses spacing, pace, screens and quick decisions to force two defenders to guard one action—then moves the ball before the defense can recover.

SPACING

Give the action room

Keep help defenders far enough away that every double-team creates an open teammate.

PAINT TOUCH

Collapse the defense

A drive, roll or post catch inside the lane can pull defenders inward and unlock kick-out passes.

SECOND SIDE

Attack the next rotation

If the first action bends the defense without producing a shot, reverse the ball and attack before everyone resets.

0.5 DECISIONS

Shoot, pass or drive

Fast decisions keep an advantage alive. Holding the ball gives the defense time to recover.

04 · STRATEGY

Every game is a set of choices.

Coaches balance lineup strengths, opponent tendencies, foul trouble, pace, score and time. A strategy is not automatically good or bad; it is a bet on which tradeoff favors your team.

01

Pace control

Run after misses and turnovers, or slow the game to protect possessions and organize half-court execution.

Ask: Who benefits from more possessions?
02

Matchup hunting

Use screens and movement to place the preferred defender in the action—or force a switch the offense wants.

Ask: Who is being made to defend repeatedly?
03

Possession math

Three-point volume, free throws, offensive rebounds and turnovers shape how efficiently a team uses its chances.

Ask: Where are the extra possessions coming from?
04

Lineup geometry

Shooting stretches the floor, size protects the rim, speed increases pressure and versatility expands coverage choices.

Ask: What did the substitution change?
05

Foul and timeout economy

Late-game choices depend on team fouls, remaining timeouts, advance-the-ball rules and who can be protected.

Ask: What resources remain?
06

Two-for-one

Near the end of a quarter, a quick first shot may create time for one more possession after the opponent attacks.

Ask: Did the first shot leave enough clock?

05 · WATCH LIKE A COACH

Watch one possession twice.

First follow the ball. On the replay, ignore it. Track the screener, the low defender and the player in the weak-side corner. That second viewing is where the hidden work appears.

YOUR FILM LOG

Four lines are enough

  1. Lineup: Who is on the floor?
  2. Action: What starts the possession?
  3. Coverage: How does the defense respond?
  4. Result: What shot or turnover follows—and why?

Try tracking five straight possessions instead of one entire game. Patterns get loud very quickly.
