October crowns do not come with nets

Preseason rankings are basketball’s seating chart. They tell us who gets the good table before anybody has served the food. ESPN’s October 13 list puts South Carolina, UConn and USC in the first three seats. That is a useful starting point. It is also an invitation to ask what would make those choices look smart in March.

Our question is simple: are we ranking a program’s reputation, a roster’s ceiling, or the team we expect to trust when a close game turns ugly? Those can be three different answers. A roster can win the introduction and still need months to learn how to win the possession.

The ranks below belong to ESPN, not an AP poll and not a We Know the W score. The watch questions and verdicts are our editorial analysis. Nobody has earned a 2026–27 regular-season résumé yet.

South Carolina: being the favorite means showing your work

South Carolina at No. 1 is defensible. It should also invite the toughest examination. Our test for Dawn Staley’s team is the quality of its half-court possessions: who organizes the offense, who gets the ball to the right spot, and who creates a good shot after the first option disappears?

A favorite does not need to look flawless in November. It needs workable answers. Watch late-clock decisions, spacing around the paint, and whether the same action can produce multiple reads instead of one predictable finish. Defensive pressure can create easy points; a championship opponent will try to take those away.

The W verdict: keep South Carolina in the first tier. Make it earn separation through possession quality. The logo is an introduction; the execution is the argument.

UConn: Sarah Strong is the foundation, not the entire building

UConn enters with Sarah Strong, the reigning national player of the year and unanimous BIG EAST preseason player of the year. KK Arnold and Blanca Quiñonez also earned unanimous preseason all-conference selections. Those are real reasons to expect a contender, confirmed by the program’s October 1 announcement.

Our film question is what happens when opponents load up on Strong. Can the next receiver attack the advantage before the defense resets? Can a second creator rescue a possession without turning it into a difficult solo shot? The supporting cast matters most when an opponent has spent a week designing a plan for the star.

For WNBA scouting, Strong’s value is not confined to scoring. Track the speed of her reads, defensive coverage changes, and decisions against help. A prospect who makes the right play when the easiest play is unavailable is telling us something useful.

USC: JuJu is back. The chemistry still has to arrive

USC confirms JuJu Watkins is returning after missing 2025–26 through injury. Both conference coaches and media named her Big Ten preseason player of the year. Jazzy Davidson joins her on the preseason all-conference teams after leading USC in points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks last season.

That makes No. 3 understandable. It does not make cohesion automatic. A returning star and an established young creator need shared timing: when to initiate, when to cut, when to clear a side, and when to turn a touch into an advantage for somebody else.

We should judge Watkins’ return through basketball evidence rather than demanding a highlight reel immediately. Watch how the offense functions across multiple games, how responsibilities are distributed, and how the group responds to physical defense. A preseason honor is recognition, not a medical clearance or a guarantee about workload.

The W verdict: USC has an exciting ceiling. The first useful question is whether its talented players can make one another easier to guard less often. Talent gathers attention. Connected talent gathers wins.

UCLA at 13: disrespect, or a different roster?

The defending champion starting at No. 13 is the obvious debate starter. UCLA’s own draft announcement supplies important context: six Bruins were selected in April, including five in the first round. That is a record-setting professional pipeline and a substantial amount of college production walking out the door.

Our read: the championship deserves full credit, while the new team deserves its own evaluation. It is reasonable to forecast uncertainty after that kind of departure. It is also reasonable to expect the coaching staff’s habits to carry over. Neither point settles what the next roster can accomplish.

Watch rebounding responsibilities, communication in transition, and which combinations remain functional when starters sit. Those details will tell us whether the Bruins are rebuilding continuity quickly. Calling every lower ranking disrespect skips the roster question; treating the title as irrelevant skips the coaching question.

The rest of the table can change the conversation

Duke and Michigan complete ESPN’s top five. Louisville and Iowa sit sixth and seventh, ahead of LSU and Texas. North Carolina closes the top ten. Our challenge to that group is the same: identify the lineup you trust against a quality opponent, then show why it works.

The lower half deserves more than a drive-by. A team ranked in the teens can become a serious threat if its complementary pieces fit faster than expected. Conversely, a top-ten roster can spend the winter working through a problem everyone politely ignored in October.

For fans, the most useful early result is not always the biggest win. Watch what happens after a team’s preferred style gets disrupted. Can it defend without fouling, survive a cold shooting stretch, and finish a possession with a rebound? That is where preseason optimism starts becoming evidence.

Who Got Next? Scout the job, not just the name

This season is also a new chapter in the WNBA pipeline. Watkins and Strong deserve attention, but scouting should not become a contest to see who can repeat the most famous names. Every prospect has to show skills that fit a professional role.

For a lead guard, watch decisions against pressure and the ability to manipulate help. For a wing, watch shooting preparation, closeout attacks and defensive assignments. For a big, watch screening angles, defensive positioning, finishing windows and passing when the paint is crowded.

College ranking, individual awards and draft position answer different questions. A freshman can reshape a contender without being part of the next draft. A veteran can be a compelling prospect on a team outside the top ten. Draft eligibility and declarations should be verified separately; being on our radar is not a declaration.

The Food for Thought takeaway: keep the rankings, keep the debate, and ask for the film. October gives us the hypothesis. The season gets to grade the homework.
