CONTRACT LANGUAGE

What exactly did she sign?

“Signed” can mean very different things. The contract type tells you how much control the team has, how long the player may stay and whether the deal uses a standard roster spot.

01

Contracts, in plain English

A player contract is the agreement that gives a team the right to employ a player under league and CBA rules. Salary, years, guarantees, options, roster status and special designations determine what happens next.

Think of it this way: “Signed” tells you she joined the organization. The contract type tells you how permanent, expensive and flexible that move is.
STANDARDRegular WNBA contract

Counts toward the standard roster and team salary rules.

ROOKIE SCALEDrafted-player structure

Young players enter under the league’s rookie contract system, with the 2026 scale substantially increased.

DEVELOPMENTALDevelopment pipeline

Two spots per team, outside the standard salary cap, with practice access and limited game activation.

HARDSHIPTemporary roster relief

Used when league-approved circumstances make the normal roster impractical because players are unavailable.

QUALIFYING OFFERFree-agency control tool

A team can preserve certain negotiating or matching rights by making the appropriate qualifying offer.

CORE OFFERVeteran control designation

A one-year fully guaranteed supermax qualifying offer tied to the Core designation.

02

Salary cap

The salary cap is the team payroll limit, not a maximum salary for one player. Teams have to fit their standard contracts within the league’s cap rules while still filling the required roster.

2026: the league announced a $7.0 million team cap, a $1.4 million maximum salary level and minimum salaries ranging from $270,000 to $300,000 based on service.
Why it matters: A team can love a player and still be unable to add her unless the contract, roster slot and cap math work together.
03

Guaranteed vs. flexible money

Not every contract has the same protection. Guarantee language affects how much salary remains owed or charged if a player is waived.

Fan translation: A guaranteed deal is harder to simply erase. A more flexible deal gives the front office more room to change the roster.
Watch the transaction wording: “waived,” “released,” “signed,” “converted” and “rest-of-season” are clues that the contract status changed.

ROSTER MATH

Why a team can need a player and still not have a spot.

A front office is managing people, money and roster categories at the same time. “How many players are on the team?” is no longer answered by one number alone.

04

Roster limits

For 2026, the WNBA says teams can roster 12 standard players and can also carry two developmental players. If a team drops below 12 standard players during the regular season, the league’s 2026 free-agency guidance says it generally has 72 hours to fill the spot.

Plain English: The 12 standard spots are the main roster. Developmental contracts are a separate lane.
Important: hardship and other league-approved exceptions can temporarily change the normal headcount.
05

Developmental contracts

The 2026 system allows two developmental roster spots. These players can practice with the team, receive stipends and benefits, and can be activated for up to 12 games.

Think “in-house pipeline,” not full standard roster spot. A developmental signing lets a team keep working with a player without treating the contract exactly like a normal standard deal.
Site connection: We Know the W keeps developmental status visible in Playerpedia and Player Movement so the player does not disappear from the roster story.
06

Hardship deals

A hardship mechanism gives a team temporary roster relief when enough players are unavailable and the league approves an exception. That can allow a team to add a replacement even when normal roster limits would otherwise block the move.

Plain English: The league is saying, “You do not have enough healthy or available players to operate normally, so you may temporarily add help.”
Why the contract can be short-lived: hardship relief exists because of the unavailable players. When the underlying hardship changes, the replacement player’s roster situation can change too. That is why a hardship signing and a later waiver can both be logical front-office moves.

FREE AGENCY

Free agent does not always mean completely free.

Years of service and the team’s qualifying offer determine how much control the previous team keeps.

07

Restricted free agent

Under the 2026 rules, a player who is out of contract with four years of WNBA service can become a restricted free agent if her team sends the required Restricted Qualifying Offer.

Plain English: She may negotiate with another team, but her previous team gets the right to match a multi-year offer sheet.
If the old team matches: the player stays on the offer-sheet terms. If it does not match, she joins the new team.
08

Reserved player

A player with three years of service or fewer can become Reserved if her previous team extends a Reserved Qualifying Offer.

Plain English: This is more restrictive than RFA. The prior team holds the exclusive negotiating rights unless it declines to make the qualifying offer.
Why fans miss this: a player can be “out of contract” but still not have the freedom of a veteran unrestricted free agent.
09

Core designation

A team can have at most one veteran free agent under the Core designation at a time. The Core Qualifying Offer is a fully guaranteed one-year supermax offer, and the designating team controls where the player can directly sign during free agency.

Closest translation: It functions somewhat like a franchise tag, but the WNBA’s rules are their own system.
Movement is still possible: the player and team can agree to a sign-and-trade. Starting in 2027, players with seven or more years of service cannot be designated as Core Players.
10

Unrestricted free agent

An unrestricted free agent can negotiate and sign with another team, subject to league rules, cap space and any special status that may apply.

Plain English: Her old team does not have RFA matching rights just because she used to play there.
But: “UFA” does not mean every team can afford the same offer or has the same roster opening.

DRAFT ROOM

Lottery luck, expansion strategy and rights.

The college draft and an expansion draft solve different problems. One assigns incoming talent. The other helps a new franchise acquire existing league players and rights.

11

WNBA Draft Lottery

The lottery determines the earliest first-round picks among eligible non-playoff teams. For the 2026 Draft Lottery, five teams participated. Fourteen numbered balls generated combinations for the No. 1 and No. 2 picks, then the remaining lottery teams were ordered at Nos. 3 through 5 by their cumulative two-season records.

Plain English: Being the worst team does not automatically guarantee the first pick. The lottery adds chance at the top, while recent record still matters.
Trade wrinkle: the team in the lottery is not always the team that owns the pick. Previously traded first-rounders can move lottery chances to another franchise.
12

Expansion Draft

An expansion draft lets a new franchise select players or rights from existing teams before its first season. In 2026, the 13 existing teams could protect up to five players for the Portland Fire and Toronto Tempo expansion draft.

2026 format: Portland and Toronto alternated over two rounds, each with up to six selections per round. No existing team could lose more than two unprotected players, up to one per round.
Not just active players: the submitted roster lists could include active, suspended, draft-list/reserved, core and retired-list rights. Open the full Expansion Draft 101 →
13

Draft picks can be assets before they become players

WNBA teams can trade draft picks. For the 2026 Draft, the league said picks could be traded until the night before the draft. Once a selection was actually made, the selected player could then be traded during or after the draft.

Plain English: “Pick No. 8” is an asset. After the name is called, the asset becomes the rights to that drafted player.
That is why draft-night graphics can change fast: a team can select a player for another team as part of an agreed transaction.

TRANSACTION DESK

Trades and waivers are different exits.

A trade sends a contract or basketball rights to another team as part of an exchange. A waiver removes a player from the roster and exposes her to the league’s waiver process.

14

Trade rules

Teams can exchange players, contracts, rights and eligible draft assets when the transaction satisfies league rules. The 2026 in-season trade deadline was August 2 at 3:00 p.m. ET.

Plain English: A trade is not just “Player A for Player B.” Cap space, roster spots, draft rights and contract status can all be part of the math.
Special case: a Core player can still move through an agreed sign-and-trade. Expansion-draft teams could also make pre-draft agreements about selecting or not selecting players.
15

Waivers

When a team waives a player, it removes her from its active roster and places her into the league’s waiver process. Another team may be able to claim her under the applicable rules. If she clears waivers, her next status depends on her contract and rights situation.

Plain English: “Waived” does not mean retired, erased or permanently out of the league.
2026 playoff note: August 24 was the league’s waiver date tied to playoff eligibility. That is one reason late-season waiver timing matters.
16

Follow the transaction from headline to roster

A move is not fully understood until you answer four questions: What happened to the player? What did the old team give up or gain? What rights does the new team now have? What changed in the roster/cap picture?

STEP 1Transaction

Signed, waived, claimed, traded, cored or converted.

STEP 2Contract

Standard, rookie, developmental, hardship or free-agent rights.

STEP 3Roster

Standard spot, developmental spot or exception.

STEP 4Cap + rights

Salary impact, matching rights, draft rights or future assets.

STEP 5Basketball impact

Who plays, who becomes available and what the rotation gains or loses.
