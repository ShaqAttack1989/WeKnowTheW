NO. 31 RISES AT HOME

Tina Charles began and ended her WNBA career in Connecticut. Now her number hangs above the same floor, preserving a record-breaking career and a relationship that became bigger than basketball.

Before Connecticut hosted Chicago on Tuesday night, Tina Charles stood beside Sun president Jennifer Rizzotti, accepted a Sun Legends varsity jacket and watched No. 31 join the names already suspended above Mohegan Sun Arena. The ceremony did not introduce her greatness. It gave that greatness a permanent address.

A full circle, not a straight line

Charles arrived in Connecticut long before she wore a Sun uniform. At UConn, she anchored back-to-back undefeated national championship teams. The Sun then selected her first overall in the 2010 WNBA Draft, keeping one of the most decorated players in college basketball close to the place where she had already learned how to win.

She was Rookie of the Year immediately. Two seasons later, Charles averaged 18.0 points and 10.5 rebounds, won league MVP and pushed Connecticut to a 25–9 record and the top seed in the Eastern Conference. She became the first Sun player to win the award.

THE FULL-CIRCLE LINEConnecticut drafted the player, watched the legend travel, and welcomed the woman home.

The years away made the return richer

Charles left after the 2013 season and built a career broad enough to touch six WNBA franchises. She played for New York, Washington, Phoenix, Seattle and Atlanta, collecting scoring titles, All-WNBA selections and records that grew larger every season.

When she returned to Connecticut in 2025, she was no longer the young center trying to establish herself. She was the league’s measuring stick for longevity. Charles led the Sun in scoring at 16.3 points per game, set a franchise single-season scoring record with 700 points and finished her career where it began.

The record book has her fingerprints everywhere

Charles retired in May as the WNBA’s all-time leader in rebounds with 4,262 and made field goals with 3,364. Her 8,396 points rank second in league history. Those totals explain the scale of the career, but they do not fully explain the consistency required to build it.

Her game lasted because it evolved. Early Charles overwhelmed defenses with power, quick second jumps and touch around the rim. Later she expanded her range, learned how to create from different spots and continued to rebound even as her role and teammates changed around her.

What Connecticut was really honoring

The banner recognizes a player, but the timing made the night about place. The Sun are spending their final Connecticut home stretch celebrating the people who formed the franchise’s identity before the organization relocates to Houston. Charles belongs in that story twice: as one of its first transcendent stars and as the veteran who chose to return for the last complete season in Uncasville.

She joined a 2026 Sun Legends class that includes Jonquel Jones, Alyssa Thomas, Jasmine Thomas and Curt Miller. No. 31 now hangs with the franchise’s other honored numbers, making sure the Connecticut chapter remains visible even after the team’s address changes.

The legacy extends beyond the glass

Charles also became a two-time recipient of the Dawn Staley Community Leadership Award. Through Hopey’s Heart Foundation, founded in memory of her aunt Maureen “Hopey” Vaz, she has helped place hundreds of automated external defibrillators in schools, recreation centers and underserved communities. In 2025, the foundation reached its 500-device milestone.

The ceremony night also carried a distinctly Tina Charles detail: she used it to introduce “SUNSET,” a hazy IPA from her 78 Brewing Co. dedicated to the players, coaches and fans who built Connecticut Sun basketball. It was remembrance turned into entrepreneurship, another way of carrying the story forward.

A banner as a promise

Jersey honors often feel like punctuation. This one feels more like preservation. Connecticut is changing, the franchise is moving and Mohegan Sun Arena will soon hold memories instead of a WNBA schedule. Raising No. 31 promises that the history will not be flattened by the move.

Charles once said that everything started for her in Connecticut. On August 25, the state answered: what she started there will remain.
