ONE MORE RUN

DeWanna Bonner is leaving Phoenix through a contract buyout, not because the relationship broke, but because the Mercury gave a franchise great room to chase a third championship while the clock still offers one more postseason.

The Phoenix Mercury and DeWanna Bonner have agreed to a contract buyout that allows the 17-year veteran to pursue a roster spot with a championship contender. Phoenix is out of the 2026 playoff race, while Bonner is staring at a postseason window that may not open many more times.

The move

The Mercury announced the agreement Monday, August 24. General manager Nick U’Ren described the decision as an act rooted in the organization’s relationship with Bonner, giving her an opportunity to chase another title in what could be her final postseason.

Bonner expressed the same uncertainty about the road beyond 2026. She did not announce her retirement. She did acknowledge that this could be “one of my final postseason opportunities,” which makes the timing of the move the story inside the story.

THE BOTTOM LINEPhoenix is protecting a relationship. Bonner is protecting one last chance.

Why the buyout makes sense

A buyout lets both sides tell the truth about where they are. The Mercury cannot reach the playoffs, so keeping a decorated veteran through the final games would preserve continuity but not create a postseason. Bonner still has enough game, experience and ambition to help a team that will be playing after Phoenix packs up.

This is also a rare example of the business of basketball bending toward the human side. Phoenix did not have to erase Bonner’s importance to make a basketball decision. The organization recognized that a player who helped hang two championship banners deserved the chance to pursue another one.

What Bonner still brings

Bonner is not being evaluated as the 20-point scorer she was at her peak. She is being evaluated as a 6-foot-4 veteran wing who has played every kind of playoff possession, can rebound her position, disrupt passing lanes and score without needing an offense built around her.

Through 37 games this season, she averaged 10.6 points, 6.1 rebounds and 1.2 steals. Those numbers make her more than a ceremonial addition. For the right contender, she can steady a second unit, close certain matchups and bring championship experience into a locker room where every late-game decision becomes heavier.

A Phoenix story, even when she leaves

The Mercury selected Bonner fifth overall in the 2009 WNBA Draft. She won three consecutive Sixth Player of the Year awards to begin her career and helped Phoenix capture championships in 2009 and 2014. After ten seasons with the Mercury, she joined Connecticut in 2020 and helped the Sun reach the 2022 Finals.

Her path eventually curved back to the desert. Bonner returned to Phoenix during the 2025 season after a brief stop in Indiana, then re-signed for 2026. This summer she crossed 8,000 career points, another marker in a career that already places her among the league’s most productive and durable players.

The buyout does not rewrite that history. If anything, it underlines it. Phoenix is not sending away a stranger. It is opening the door for family.

What happens next

No destination had been confirmed when this story was published. The next team will need more than a recognizable name. It will need a real role, a roster opening and a belief that Bonner’s playoff experience can solve a specific problem.

The cleanest fit will be a contender looking for size on the wing, defensive flexibility, rebounding and a veteran who will not be surprised by the speed or pressure of a postseason series. Bonner’s next jersey remains unknown. Her purpose does not.

The landing spot board: watch New York

Sports Illustrated identified Atlanta, Minnesota and New York as the three most logical destinations. Atlanta has the cleanest cap situation, Minnesota offers a proven championship system, and New York has the combination that makes this prediction especially interesting: available money, a playoff berth and an immediate opening for experienced frontcourt depth.

The Liberty have $129,904 in available cap space, according to Sports Illustrated’s analysis. Satou Sabally is also away from the team indefinitely while dealing with lingering effects of a head injury. Her health comes first, but her absence leaves New York without the versatile size it expected to carry into the postseason.

Our read: Atlanta may be the simplest fit on paper, but New York is the team to watch. The Liberty have a clearer immediate need, enough cap space and the kind of veteran core that lets Bonner contribute without pretending she must still be the first option.

No agreement with New York or any other team was confirmed at publication. Bonner must first pass through waivers before she can sign as an unrestricted free agent.

The bigger meaning

There is a difference between leaving and being discarded. Bonner’s exit feels closer to a franchise handing one of its legends the ball with the clock running down.

Maybe this becomes the final chapter. Maybe Bonner writes another one after it. For now, she is choosing possibility, and the Mercury are letting one of their own chase the only ending that could make a farewell feel less like goodbye: one more run, one more postseason and one more shot at a ring.
