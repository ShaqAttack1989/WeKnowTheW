THE FALL OF THE LYNX

Minnesota did not get upset. Minnesota got solved.

An upset can be a hot shooting night, a strange whistle or one bad fourth quarter. This was not that. New York won both games by the same 16-point margin. The Liberty took Game 1 in Minneapolis, then led wire to wire in Game 2. The top seed had 80 minutes to change the story. It never found the edit.

Jonquel Jones moved the series into the paint.

Jones scored New York's first seven points in Game 2 and kept collecting the misses Minnesota needed to end possessions. Six of her 13 rebounds came on the offensive glass. The Liberty won paint points 44-28 and rebounds 36-29. Minnesota's guards kept meeting size at the rim, and Minnesota's bigs kept having to defend a second action after the first stop should have ended the trip.

This was the matchup pressure behind Olivia Miles' nightmare. The unanimous Rookie of the Year went 1-for-8, finished with five points and one assist, and played only 17 minutes. Across the series, she scored 16 points with two assists while shooting 20 percent. That does not erase her brilliant season. It is also exactly the kind of film every contender will place on her desk next spring.

New York's stars looked like stars.

Breanna Stewart played all 40 minutes and filled every column that mattered: 21 points, five rebounds, four assists, two steals and two blocks. Sabrina Ionescu shook off an uneven season with 18 points, seven assists and four threes. Jones finished with 21 and 13. The trio scored 60 of New York's 87 points and scored or assisted on 70.

Minnesota had one clear answer. Courtney Williams brought 18 points, eight rebounds and five assists. Kayla McBride gave the Lynx 14 points and three steals. But one good backcourt night cannot cover a frontcourt mismatch, a rookie point guard trapped in traffic and a franchise star unable to bend the game.

The regular season built a résumé. The series demanded counters.

Cheryl Reeve earned Coach of the Year by guiding a remade roster to 33 wins while Collier missed the first half of the season. That work is real. So is the failure to produce a playoff adjustment that changed the geometry. New York shot 52 percent from three in Game 1. The Liberty dropped to 31 percent in Game 2 and still won by 16 because Jones owned the extra possessions, Stewart controlled the pace and Minnesota scored fewer than 20 points in each of the last three quarters.

The ugliest number is not 71. It is zero. Minnesota never led in Game 2. The league's best regular-season team spent its elimination night chasing.

THE SHAK TAKE

New York was an eighth seed by record, not by ceiling. Minnesota knew that before the series. It still played as if the better seed would eventually rescue the better season.

This is more than a bad week.

The Lynx lost the 2024 Finals, fell in the 2025 semifinals and just exited in the 2026 first round. Each season had its own context. Together, they create a direction. Minnesota is still a first-class organization with an elite coach, a foundational rookie and a superstar. But the postseason trend is moving backward while Collier's leverage is moving forward.

SEMIFINAL FORMAT FIRST TO 3

No WNBA team has rallied from 0-2 to win a playoff series. New York must make history to advance.

01

The “eight seed” label is camouflage.

New York went 26-18, won the regular-season series against Minnesota and still carries the Jones-Stewart-Ionescu championship core. No remaining team should treat the Liberty like a traditional bottom seed.

02

Best-of-three rewards the first punch.

The current 1-1-1 opening round leaves almost no recovery window. Minnesota lost its home opener and had to save the season in Brooklyn 48 hours later.

03

Atlanta has seized the wider road.

The only 33-win team is gone, and the Dream have turned that opening into a 2-0 lead over New York. One more win sends Atlanta to the Finals.

The honest answer is that nobody outside her circle knows. The important answer is that she can.

BOTTOM LINE

Do not turn a long postgame hug into a transaction. Collier has not requested a trade, announced a departure or publicly closed the door on Minnesota. She chose a one-year deal, called herself “focused on the now” and noted that contracts create “new opportunities every year.” The Lynx should hear that as leverage, not betrayal.

MINNESOTA'S OFFSEASON BOARD

What must change before the sales pitch begins.

01

Build a playoff offense, not just a winning offense.

Minnesota needs counters when length closes the paint and opponents sit on Miles' first read.

02

Give Collier a frontcourt answer.

Jones turned one miss into two possessions all night. Size, rebounding and interior physicality cannot be a one-player job.

03

Keep developing Miles without hiding the film.

The rookie season was special. The series exposed the next syllabus: pressure reads, pull-up balance and passing over size.

04

Make the pitch before free agency.

Collier already knows Minnesota's culture. The front office must show how the basketball plan will stop repeating the same ending.
