THE COACHING DEBATE

There is no single kind of greatness.

Winning a title is the loudest credential. It is not the whole résumé. Some coaches build dynasties; some change a franchise’s ceiling; some turn a first-year expansion team into a playoff team. We score those stories separately, and keep regular-season records, playoff records and championships visible beside every number.

All grades use a shared 55–99 peer scale, with a separate comparison pool and career inputs for each board. The WNBA grade compares the selected WNBA coaches; the college grade compares the selected Division I coaches. They are not cross-league equivalents.

THE FOOD FOR THOUGHT QUESTION

Was Smesko robbed?

2025 COACH OF THE YEAR

No.

Nakase earned 53 of 72 votes; Smesko finished second with 15. In year one, Nakase took an expansion roster to a 23–21 record and the Valkyries became the first expansion team to reach the playoffs in its inaugural season. That was a historic award case, not a bad call.

2026 COACH OF THE YEAR

Overlooked? Yes. Robbed? Debatable.

The official ballot gave Reeve 40 votes, Nakase 20 and Sydney Johnson 14. Those 74 votes leave Smesko with none, despite Atlanta’s 30–14 regular season. That is a fair snub argument. Reeve still had the league’s best record at 33–11, while Nakase went 32–12. The Finals run came after the regular-season award was decided, so it cannot be the evidence for changing that ballot.

Our call: Nakase was the right 2025 winner. In 2026, Smesko deserved a place in the conversation; “robbed” depends on whether you think Atlanta’s 30-win season should have earned him some share of the 74 votes.

FOOD FOR THOUGHT

What should count most in a coaching legacy?

Make the playoffs. It is the minimum test of a regular-season builder.

Win the games that get harder. Postseason results deserve their own column.

Change the ceiling. A first-year expansion playoff berth and a fourth title are not the same achievement, but both can be extraordinary.

Who belongs above the numbers: the dynasty builder, the best tactician, or the coach who changed what a franchise could be?
