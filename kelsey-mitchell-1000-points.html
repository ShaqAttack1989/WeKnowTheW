THE 1,000 POINT GUARD

Kelsey Mitchell crossed a scoring line no WNBA guard had reached before. Her 34 point night against Connecticut pushed her to 1,001 points for the season and into a two player club with A’ja Wilson.

Kelsey Mitchell did not merely reach four digits. She changed who can be pictured when we talk about a 1,000 point WNBA season.

The first guard through the door

Mitchell scored 34 points in Indiana’s August 28 win over Connecticut, lifting her 2026 total to 1,001. That made her the first guard in league history to score at least 1,000 points in a single season and only the second player at any position to do it.

A’ja Wilson established the 1,000 point season club in 2024. Mitchell joining her matters because the routes are different. Wilson created history from the frontcourt. Mitchell reached the same threshold as a 5 foot 8 guard whose scoring is built on pace, pull ups, three point volume, movement and relentless pressure on defenses.

Volume without giving away efficiency

At the moment she crossed the mark, Mitchell had played 40 games and was averaging 25.0 points while shooting 51.3 percent from the field and 45.5 percent from three. Reaching 1,000 is a volume achievement, but those percentages show why this season is more than accumulation.

Her August 28 line fit the larger pattern. Mitchell made 12 of 19 shots against Connecticut, scored 34, and helped Indiana turn an early deficit into a 111 to 91 win.

The record is still moving

The 1,001 total is a milestone snapshot, not a final season number. Mitchell’s 2026 scoring total will continue to change as Indiana plays. What will not change is the first: no WNBA guard had ever crossed 1,000 points in one season before her.

That is why this belongs in Milestone Moments, not only in a nightly box score. Mitchell moved the scoring ceiling for her position.
