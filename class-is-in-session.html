W-READY SKILLS

What actually travels?

College dominance is not one-size-fits-all. This board focuses on skills that tend to survive the jump: creation against length, quick decisions, shooting gravity, defensive versatility, rebounding and the ability to thrive without owning every possession.

Film-room rule: a W-ready skill is not a guarantee of WNBA success. It is a reason to keep watching how that skill holds up against better size, speed and scouting.

THE LONG CLASS LIST

Women’s college basketball built the audience before it got the credit.

This section keeps the scouting desk connected to the larger game: championship eras, tournament growth, star pipelines and the programs that helped make women’s basketball impossible to ignore.

1982
First NCAA women’s basketball tournament

The NCAA begins sponsoring its Division I women’s championship, creating the modern tournament record book.

1997
A direct path into a new pro league

The WNBA’s launch gives college stars a new domestic professional destination and makes draft translation part of the college conversation.

2022
Women finally get “March Madness” branding

The NCAA extends the March Madness name to the women’s tournament, aligning public branding with the event’s actual stature.

2024+
Audience growth changes the business

Record-setting attention, NIL and stronger pro pathways make player development, retention and draft decisions more visible than ever.

2027
March Madness expands to 76 teams

The women’s tournament adds 12 Opening Round games at campus sites before the 64-team First Round.
