FOOD FOR THOUGHT

Return night did not settle the race. It made it louder.

There was a reasonable question hanging over September 17: what would the playoff teams look like after the World Cup break? Atlanta, Washington, Dallas and Las Vegas answered it with a combined 424 points and not one close finish.

Atlanta did not return quietly.

Angel Reese needed less than 25 minutes to put up 30 points and 10 rebounds in a 103–59 win over Connecticut. Rhyne Howard missed her first eight attempts from three and still found the shot that mattered historically: No. 129, the new WNBA single-season record. Atlanta also got a franchise scoring record from Allisha Gray. The larger playoff point is not that every Dream star shot perfectly. It is that Atlanta won by 44 without needing perfection from any one of them.

Jackie Young brought the gold-medal heat home.

Las Vegas saved the loudest closing statement for Seattle. Young scored 22 in the first quarter and 35 for the game as the Aces won 114–77. A'ja Wilson added 29 points, seven rebounds and five assists. If the concern was whether the international turnaround would dull Las Vegas' edge, the first quarter answered before most people had settled into the late window.

Washington's young core looked ready for the next assignment.

The Mystics beat Chicago 110–80 behind 20 points from Shakira Austin and an 18-point, 11-rebound double-double from Kiki Iriafen. Washington's value here was not just the margin. It was the shape of the win: five starters in double figures, efficient offense and a defense that turned Chicago mistakes into immediate pressure.

Dallas keeps removing the word “surprise.”

The Wings beat Los Angeles 97–77 for a fifth straight win. Paige Bueckers scored 19, while Jessica Shepard posted 13 points, 11 rebounds and 12 assists for her fifth triple-double of the season. Dallas tied the franchise single-season wins record. At some point, a hot streak becomes a basketball identity, and Dallas is getting very close to that line.

Phoenix and Portland gave the night its thriller.

The only game without a playoff team was also the only game decided by one possession. Phoenix beat Portland 94–91 behind 22 bench points from Shay Ciezki and an 18-point, eight-rebound, six-assist line from Alyssa Thomas. Those teams are out of the postseason race, but September still matters when a franchise is deciding which habits and players belong in the next version of itself.

The real takeaway

Return night was not proof of what will happen in the playoffs. It was proof that the teams with postseason business did not need a grace period. Atlanta, Washington, Dallas and Las Vegas all scored at least 97. Three reached 100. None won by fewer than 20. The FIBA medals are already handed out. Now the currency is seeding, home court and rhythm.

SEPTEMBER 18 · THE AFTERSHOCK

The weekend turned the volume up again.

Thursday established the tone. The weekend made the seeding race tighter. Golden State kept the pressure on at the top, Dallas stretched its winning streak to six, Atlanta created a little room in the home-court race, and Minnesota answered Sunday by moving to 32–10.

NEW YORK · 25–16 · NO. 6 LIBERTY 93 · LYNX 81

Seven Liberty players came back from the World Cup, and New York immediately beat Minnesota on the road. Breanna Stewart posted 24 points and 10 rebounds, Sabrina Ionescu added 17 points and six assists, Pauline Astier scored 14, and Marine Johannès scored 13. Minnesota fell to 31–10 and a three-game losing streak.

INDIANA · 27–14 · NO. 5 FEVER 103 · TEMPO 85

Indiana moved level with Atlanta in record and kept the fight for the No. 4 seed and first-round home court alive. The Dream still sits fourth in the current order, so every remaining result matters.

GOLDEN STATE · 30–11 · NO. 2 VALKYRIES 82 · FIRE 66

Golden State clinched first-round home court and moved within one game of Minnesota for the No. 1 seed. The Valkyries did it short-handed, with several World Cup returnees unavailable, while Kaitlyn Chen and Laeticia Amihere each scored 17.

Sunday check-in: Minnesota is 32–10 after beating Connecticut 101–89. Golden State is 31–11 and still chasing the top seed. Atlanta is 28–14, Dallas is 26–16 on a six-game winning streak, and the rest of the middle is still moving as Sunday’s games finish. Everybody has a playoff ticket. Nobody has their seat assignment yet.
