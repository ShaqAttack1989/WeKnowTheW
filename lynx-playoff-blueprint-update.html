SO, ABOUT THAT
LYNX BLUEPRINT...

Golden State showed one way to bother Minnesota. Atlanta just showed another, and this time Courtney Williams was back.

By We Know the W Staff5-minute readSeptember 2, 2026
Home›Snack Shak›Food for Thought›Lynx Blueprint

I wanted to see Minnesota against Atlanta before getting too comfortable with the idea of a Lynx playoff blueprint. We got the test, and Atlanta gave the conversation a second chapter.

The Dream beat Minnesota 89–81 on August 30. More important than the final score, Courtney Williams was back. That matters because her absence was the biggest asterisk hanging over Golden State's 80–66 win. The Lynx had another ball handler again and still couldn't take control of the game.

Atlanta didn't copy Golden State

That's probably the most useful part of this result. Golden State bothered Minnesota with ball pressure, delayed offense and turnovers. Atlanta found another route.

The Dream won the rebounding battle 37–32. Angel Reese finished with 15 points and 11 rebounds. Jordin Canada had 16 points and 10 assists. Atlanta kept Minnesota defending, moved the ball and found enough offense from different places that the Lynx couldn't simply load up on one player.

THE NEW FILM NOTEYou don't have to beat Minnesota one specific way. You have to keep taking comfortable possessions away from them.

Extra possessions come in different packages

Golden State stole possessions with turnovers. Atlanta stole them on the glass. The box score calls those different things, but in a playoff game the effect is pretty similar. Minnesota gets fewer clean chances to settle into its offense, while the opponent gets another opportunity to score.

That's why the rebounding piece keeps moving higher on my list. If you're not a team built to hound the ball for 40 minutes, you can still make Minnesota work by extending possessions and making the Lynx defend again.

Then Atlanta made the math hurt

The Dream hit 13 threes. Minnesota made six. That's a 21-point swing from behind the line in a game Atlanta won by eight.

Allisha Gray made six of them and scored 23. Rhyne Howard scored 12 of her 16 points in the fourth quarter. That last part is worth remembering. Minnesota doesn't just need to be uncomfortable early. Somebody has to make the Lynx pay late.

Howard did.

The Courtney Williams asterisk got smaller

Williams returned from her right-foot sprain and scored 11. Her presence didn't magically erase the problems we've been watching.

That changes the conversation. The Golden State lesson was never supposed to be “pressure Olivia Miles because Williams is missing.” The better version is broader: make Minnesota defend multiple points of attack, keep the Lynx from controlling possession after possession, and force their offense to work later into the clock than it wants to.

There is a new availability question, though. Miles took a hard fall late against Atlanta. Until Minnesota provides something definitive, I'm not turning that into an injury diagnosis. It is simply something to watch as the postseason gets closer.

Two different losses, one bigger picture

Golden State gave us the defensive version. Pressure the guards. Create turnovers. Get real production from the bench. Make Minnesota spend time just trying to begin its offense.

Atlanta gave us the possession version. Rebound. Move the ball. Make the Lynx guard several creators. Get into the paint enough to move the defense, then punish the rotations from three. And when the fourth quarter arrives, have somebody who can actually finish the job.

The scary playoff opponent is the team that can do some of both.

Food for thought

Minnesota is still 31–9 and still sitting on top of the league. Two losses don't erase months of evidence that the Lynx are very, very good.

But the question has changed.

We aren't asking whether one weird Golden State night exposed something. Now we've seen two different opponents make Minnesota uncomfortable in two different ways, including one with Williams back in the lineup.

Pressure the ball when you can. Win the glass. Create extra possessions. Get something from the bench. Touch the paint. Make Minnesota rotate. Hit the open three. And if you're lucky enough to still have the game in front of you late, you better have somebody ready to take it.

Golden State gave the league the first page. Atlanta just wrote in the margins.
