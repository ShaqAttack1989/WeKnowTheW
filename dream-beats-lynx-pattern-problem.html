Two games ago, Minnesota could call it one ugly night. After Atlanta's 89 to 81 win Sunday, that explanation has officially expired.

The Dream took the rubber match of the season series, 2 to 1, improved to 26 and 14, and sent the league leading Lynx into the World Cup break at 31 and 9 with consecutive losses for the first time since early July. Minnesota is still Minnesota. It still owns the best record. Nobody needs to hit a panic button. But somebody should probably move the button within reach.

Because the most interesting part of this two game slide is that Golden State and Atlanta did not beat the Lynx the same way.

Atlanta won this one before halftime

The Dream opened with a 26 to 21 first quarter, then repeated the pressure with another 26 points in the second. By halftime, Atlanta led 52 to 41.

Minnesota actually won the second half 40 to 37. That is the sort of detail that can make a final score look more competitive than the game felt. The Lynx spent the night trying to repair damage that had already been done.

THE GAME IN ONE NUMBERAtlanta made seven more threes than Minnesota. Seven extra makes from deep equals 21 points of scoreboard leverage in an eight point win.

The Dream did not copy Golden State. They translated the idea.

Our last Lynx film piece asked whether Atlanta could take pieces of Golden State's pressure blueprint and make them fit its own personnel. The answer was yes, but the box score makes an important distinction.

Golden State beat Minnesota by speeding up decisions and forcing 18 Lynx turnovers. Atlanta did almost the opposite in the turnover column. The Dream committed 18 turnovers themselves while Minnesota had only 11. If this were simply a copy and paste job, Atlanta should have lost.

Instead, the Dream attacked different pressure points. They stretched the floor, won the rebounding battle and made Minnesota's best rookie scorer operate more like an isolated scorer than the connective playmaker who usually organizes the Lynx offense.

Allisha Gray controlled the temperature

Allisha Gray finished with 23 points and 11 rebounds, hit six of her 11 three point attempts, added three steals and finished plus 17. Every time Minnesota threatened to make the game small again, Atlanta had a perimeter answer available.

Jordin Canada added 16 points and 10 assists. Rhyne Howard scored 16 with three assists and no turnovers. That matters because Minnesota's defense still created chaos. Atlanta just had enough creation across multiple guards to survive it.

Miles scored. Atlanta disconnected the rest.

Olivia Miles led Minnesota with 21 points. That is the good news. The number sitting beside those 21 points is the one Atlanta will probably circle in red during film review: zero assists.

Miles entered Sunday averaging 6.1 assists per game. Against the Dream, she did not record one. That does not mean she played poorly. She shot 7 of 14, got to the line seven times and kept attacking. It means Atlanta made her scoring live separately from the easy creation Minnesota usually gets around it.

Napheesa Collier and Kayla McBride each scored 17. Natasha Howard filled the stat sheet with nine points, seven rebounds and seven assists. But Minnesota finished with only 17 team assists, while Atlanta had 21. The Lynx had production. The Dream had flow.

Angel Reese put a record inside the win

Reese finished with 15 points and 11 rebounds, giving her 29 double doubles this season. She entered the game tied with Alyssa Thomas at 28 for the WNBA single season record. Now the record belongs to Reese alone.

And this was not history detached from winning. Atlanta outrebounded Minnesota 37 to 32. Reese had three offensive rebounds, eight defensive rebounds, two steals and a block. She helped make sure Minnesota's defensive possessions actually ended.

That is the Atlanta version of the blueprint. Golden State manufactured extra possessions through pressure. Atlanta created its advantage with spacing, shooting and the glass.

The second straight loss changes the conversation

Back to back losses do not erase a 31 win season. They do, however, create better playoff questions.

Golden State showed that Minnesota can be pushed out of its preferred timing. Atlanta showed that the Lynx can also be made to chase a game when an opponent has enough shooting and rebounding to punish every slow start. Those are two different routes to the same destination.

The Dream also won the season series 2 to 1. Atlanta took the opener 91 to 90, Minnesota answered 96 to 81 in May, and Sunday's win gave the final word to the Dream before the break.

OLIVIA MILES HEALTH WATCHConcern, yes. Diagnosis, not yet.

There was late concern around Miles' health, but no new official postgame diagnosis was available when this article was published. That distinction matters. Miles recently twisted her left ankle against Golden State on August 19 and did not return to that game, then returned to action afterward. Until Minnesota provides an update from Sunday, this belongs in the monitor column, not the confirmed injury column.

Now Minnesota has to sit with it

The timing might be the most uncomfortable part. The WNBA pauses for the FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup, so Minnesota does not get an immediate chance to wash this taste out with another game. The Lynx are scheduled to return September 18 against New York.

That also gives Cheryl Reeve and her staff something valuable: time. Time to look at the Golden State tape next to the Atlanta tape. Time to decide which problems are noise and which are becoming repeatable. Time to get healthier.

Food for thought

Minnesota is still the team everybody has to catch. But being the best team over 40 games and being the hardest team to beat four times in a playoff series are related questions, not identical ones.

Golden State made Minnesota uncomfortable by shrinking its time. Atlanta made Minnesota uncomfortable by stretching its defense and winning the math from three. Two teams, two methods, two straight losses.

That is not a collapse. It is a scouting report getting thicker.
