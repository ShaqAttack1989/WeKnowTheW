THE BIG IDEA

Money matters. Choice matters more.

The most important names in this class are not all chasing the same thing. Some have championship continuity. Some can finally choose their environment. Some are staring at smaller roles than their talent suggests. That is why the same dollar figure can mean very different things to different players.

Why Golden State and Washington keep appearing

Golden State finished 32-12 with the league's best opponent scoring mark but ranked 14th in scoring and 13th in assists, making another elite creator an obvious basketball fit. Washington went 28-16 with the No. 2 opponent scoring mark, while ranking 12th in scoring and 11th in assists; adding a veteran shot-creator or organizer next to its young core would attack the clearest offensive weakness. Seattle's 8-36 finish and bottom-tier scoring/playmaking numbers make it a very different pitch: less instant title equity, more opportunity to become the veteran offensive anchor of a rebuild.

THE FOUR DOMINOES

The stars who can redraw the map.

LEAN STAY

Napheesa Collier

Minnesota still offers an elite basketball situation and a familiar home base. The difference in 2027 is leverage: Collier can listen to everybody before deciding whether home is still the best basketball answer.

MOVE WATCH

Kelsey Mitchell

Indiana can sell winning, continuity and a huge stage. Mitchell can finally decide whether she wants that exact stage again. That makes her the most compelling change-of-scenery candidate among the top stars.

TRUE TOSS-UP

Kelsey Plum

Phoenix invested to get the first recruiting window, but the roster still has to prove the fit is worth extending. Plum has already shown she will make the decision that works for her, not for the neatest storyline.

LIKELY STAY

Jackie Young

Other contenders should call because they have to. Vegas still has the cleanest pitch: role clarity, title equity and A'ja Wilson. A departure would be one of the biggest surprises of the market.

FOOD FOR THOUGHT

Kelsey Mitchell is the pressure point.

If Mitchell stays, Indiana keeps one of the league's most dangerous backcourts together and the market loses one of its best scorers. If she leaves, every team with cap room and a playoff timeline suddenly has permission to dream bigger. That single decision could set the price and pace for the rest of the guard market.

The sleeper team to watch: Washington. A young playoff roster that needs more perimeter creation is exactly the kind of situation that turns a theoretical free-agent pitch into a credible one.
