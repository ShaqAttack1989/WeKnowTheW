THE NAME BEFORE THE NUMBERS

A jersey introduces the team before the scoreboard does.

Say “Dream” and you might picture a championship parade. Atlanta’s name asks you to look further back, to Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and a promise much larger than basketball. Say “Wings” and dinner might cross your mind. Dallas is thinking Pegasus. The menu can wait.

A team name is a tiny piece of public storytelling. It has to fit across a jersey, survive a chant and give people something to claim. In the W, those few letters can carry a city’s landmarks, its history or the future a franchise wants to build.

Some names belong to the place. Others ask the place to dream.

Liberty makes New York recognizable without a map. Chicago’s Sky lifts your eyes toward its skyline. Minnesota’s Lynx draws from the natural world, while Seattle’s Storm turns regional weather into competitive personality. These names give a team local roots before it has a signature player.

Atlanta adds another layer: civic memory. A name rooted in civil rights invites a question that a win-loss record cannot settle. How does a franchise honor the promise it wears? That is an editorial question, not a claim that a name alone guarantees community impact.

The mythology has range.

Golden State’s warrior women and Dallas’ winged horse make imagination part of the uniform. Washington’s magical name also has a practical family connection to the Wizards. Cleveland’s future Sirens look toward Lake Erie. Myth can give a franchise an instant visual language, but the local connection is what helps it stick.

There is a difference between a name’s documented origin and what fans hear in it now. Sparks sounds electric. That does not establish who first proposed it or why it won. A good origin story needs receipts, even when the imagined version sounds better.

A move changes the name. It does not erase the miles.

The Wings’ trail runs through Detroit and Tulsa. The Aces’ franchise passed through Utah and San Antonio. Connecticut’s Sun followed the Orlando Miracle. A new identity can welcome a new city while leaving longtime fans with an older name stitched into their memories.

Portland requires a different distinction. The Fire name returned for 2026, but the expansion franchise is separate from the original 2000–2002 team. Cleveland’s Sirens are likewise a new franchise, rather than a renamed continuation of the Rockers. Shared cities and revived names do not automatically create shared record books.

Houston’s return carries two histories.

The Comets are essential to this conversation. Their space-themed name paired naturally with the Rockets, but Cynthia Cooper, Sheryl Swoopes and Tina Thompson helped make it mean something bigger than an NBA connection. Four straight titles gave Houston the W’s first dynasty.

The original team folded after 2008. The Comets’ approved return in 2027 brings the name back through the Sun’s relocation. That is a revival of a beloved identity and the next chapter of the Orlando–Connecticut franchise, rather than a new expansion slot. Houston fans get their name back without either history needing to disappear.

The best name still needs people.

Tempo offers a useful closing thought: an identity can be built around movement, sound and belonging. The name was designed to work in French and English. That choice helps a Canadian franchise speak to more than one audience from the start.

Our take? The strongest names give fans something to discover after they learn the chant. Players supply the highlights. Communities supply the meaning. A logo opens the door; the people decide whether it becomes home.

Reinvention · Space City

Houston Comets

Original debut: 1997 · Name returns: 2027

Space City gets its Comets back

Comets joined Houston’s space-themed sports vocabulary alongside the NBA’s Rockets when the WNBA launched in 1997. The name fits Space City, but Houston’s women built a legacy all their own: the original Comets won the first four WNBA championships, from 1997 through 2000.

The approved 2027 return comes through the Connecticut Sun’s relocation. It revives the Houston name, while the original Comets’ four titles remain part of a separate franchise’s history.

Name trail: Original Houston Comets (1997–2008, separate franchise) · Orlando Miracle → Connecticut Sun → Houston Comets (2027 return)
