THE OPENING READ

The best four teams are here. The biggest numbers mostly are not.

That is not a contradiction. It is the clearest explanation of how a short international tournament works.

The semifinalists are first through fourth in the live We Know the W Score, yet their players occupy only three of the 18 top-three positions across FIBA’s six headline leaderboards. Gabby Williams appears in efficiency and steals. Caitlin Clark appears in assists. Everyone else at the top is already out.

The Final Four has not been built by four solo acts. France bends the floor with shooting and waves of pressure. USA buries teams beneath lineups. Germany protects the ball, rebounds and lets Berlin supply the extra pulse. Spain has trusted Iyana Martin and Awa Fam with responsibilities much larger than their résumés.

WHAT EACH GAME IS REALLY ASKING

Two semifinals. Two different kinds of pressure.

One game is about whether a system can outlast a building. The other is about whether control can keep a wave from forming.

SEMIFINAL 01 · FRANCE vs GERMANY

Can Germany make France play one possession at a time?

France has spent this tournament removing oxygen. It is shooting 61.4 percent on twos and 45.7 percent from three while holding opponents to 27.6 percent beyond the arc. When Gabby Williams turns one mistake into transition and Marine Johannès stretches the defense another step, the opponent can lose the game in a two-minute blur.

Germany’s counter is not to win a track meet. It is to refuse to donate possessions. The hosts average only 9.8 turnovers, own a 46.2 to 39.5 rebounding advantage over France, and have won four straight since Spain beat them by 30 on opening day. Leonie Fiebich can control a game without dominating shots; Frieda Bühner supplies efficient scoring; Nyara Sabally just punished Belgium with 16 points and 12 rebounds.

THE PRESSURE POINTFrance wants multiplication. Germany wants preservation.

If the hosts keep the possession count clean and the crowd engaged, the favorite has to execute instead of simply overwhelm.

SEMIFINAL 02 · SPAIN vs USA

Can Spain keep USA from becoming an avalanche?

USA’s 108 to 56 quarterfinal win was not merely a response to the close call against Italy. It was a warning. Five players reached double figures, the team produced 31 assists, and the score became the highest ever in a Women’s World Cup quarterfinal. USA also poured in 59 first-half points, a quarterfinal record. It does not need one scorer to own the night because the next lineup can change the terms.

Spain cannot trade runs. FIBA’s semifinal preview gives USA a 17.3 to 8.8 edge in fast-break points and a 48.8 to 29 advantage in bench scoring. Spain’s route is deliberate: protect the ball, make the game live in the half court and trust Martin and Fam to create without feeding USA’s transition. The underdog does not need forty perfect minutes. It needs forty connected ones.

THE PRESSURE POINTSpain wants a negotiation. USA wants a landslide.

The longer the score stays tight, the more each empty American possession starts to feel expensive.

SHAK’S READ · BEFORE TIPOFF

The safest final is France vs USA. The interesting part is why that safety can fail.

France and USA own the top two W Scores, the two largest average margins and the deepest answers. That makes them the responsible picks. It does not make either semifinal ceremonial.

Germany has the best upset ingredients against a high-speed favorite: few turnovers, superior rebounding and a home crowd that has already watched one giant fall. Spain has the best upset blueprint against USA: shrink the possession game, keep the ball out of transition and make the favorite solve pressure late.

If the favorites advance, it will be because their depth turns forty minutes into too many questions. If one does not, it will be because the underdog made the game smaller until one answer was enough.
